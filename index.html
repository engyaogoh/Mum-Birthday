Once upon a time

Before she was Mum, she was a girl. A sister. A daughter. A young woman who married Dad at twenty-five — and then everything she did, she did for us.

Three children. Three very different stories. One mother who saw each of us clearly enough to give exactly what each of us needed.

Xiao Yen
What she taught us: to flourish, to want boldly, to see possibilities.

You didn't necessarily have every opportunity growing up. Being the youngest in your family, more resources and opportunities sometimes went to the older siblings, and you were the only one who didn't get the chance to go abroad. But you never let what you didn't have define what you could become. Instead, you took whatever was in your hands and found a way to make it flourish.

You have always been a woman of wisdom, with a clear and strategic mind — you always think ahead and see possibilities. I especially love that spark in your eyes whenever a new idea or perspective excites you. You are ambitious, curious, and always looking forward.

You didn't necessarily have everything growing up — but you never let what you didn't have define what you could become.

2
The youngest daughter · the happy nut
Xiao Ting
What she taught us: to hold things up with love, and to keep going even through fear.

Since I have memory, Mum has been the steady mum who seems to know everything in this world. All the missing items can be found by Mum. Broken things are fixed by Mum and Dad. We baked cakes, cooked meals, made bak zhang, and sewed cross-stitch together — Mom is the heart of our family, guiding us with strength and love.

Always ready to do anything that comes into mind. After growing up, I realised Mum really had boundless energy to do all kinds of things.

Mum was ever strong and resilient — until the illness came into her life in our early teens. We were worried and feared losing our mum, but we never talked about it. We just wanted to believe it would turn out okay.

It was a difficult time for Mum, mentally and physically — yet there she was, holding on for us anyway. She braved through the ordeal. That is where we come from. That resilience isn't just hers anymore — she gave it to us.

3
The middle child · the son
Eng Yao
What she taught us: to plan three steps ahead, and to be held.

If someone asked me who shaped my life the most, I wouldn't have to think twice. It's you. My earliest memories are all about being held. I was the most fearful child among my siblings, and you always had your arms open for me — steady, waiting, unconditional.

"Does Mama ever lie to you?" As a child, that was all the proof I needed to believe anything you said.

I think I gave you more grey hairs than most — the timid boy, the poor grades. And yet your response wasn't to panic. It was to plan. You taught me to bake and to cook, so that if I ever struggled to find my place, I'd have something to fall back on. That confidence carried me through far more than baking — it carried me through life.

The serenity prayer you sewed and framed above my bed — that wasn't decoration. Every night, that quiet reminder was there, and I've whispered it to myself more times than I can count, especially when I needed courage. Thank you for praying over me, always.

Do you remember the nights we burned the midnight oil together, because I couldn't finish my homework and was terrified of being scolded the next day? You sacrificed your sleep to sit with me and teach me. Because of that, I learned something that's stayed with me ever since: “I can do it — sometimes I just need a little more time.”

Every one of those decisions shaped the life I now have. That was never luck, Mum. That was you.

One mother · three chapters · endless love

You didn't just love us — you equipped us, in exactly the way each of us needed. Quietly. Patiently. And always, always, with foresight.

We are so lucky that you are our Mum. So proud to be your children. So grateful for everything you have given us — the skills, the laughter, and the love we carry everywhere we go.

Three of the most important things in life: faith, good health, and freedom.

May the years ahead bring you more places to explore, more people to laugh with, more ideas that put that spark in your eyes — and, most importantly, more moments that make you genuinely happy.
